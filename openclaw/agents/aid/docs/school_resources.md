# Georgia State University: money help resources

*Demo content for BidBy. The "Paying your balance" and "Georgia State Payment Plan" sections summarize Georgia State's published Student Financial Services pages (retrieved October 18, 2026). Every other section, office detail and amount is made up for the demo.*

## Paying your balance (Student Financial Services)
Source: https://sfs.gsu.edu/resources/student-acct/how-to-pay/
- You must have a payment arrangement in place by the payment due date, or your class registration may be canceled.
- The two arrangements are: pay the balance in full, or enroll in the Georgia State Payment Plan.
- Payments and plan enrollment are both done in PantherPay, through PAWS.
- Student Financial Services: 404-413-2600, or a virtual appointment from their website.

## Georgia State Payment Plan
Source: https://sfs.gsu.edu/resources/student-acct/how-to-pay/georgia-state-payment-plan/
- Defers up to 50% of the term's tuition and mandatory fees, or 50% of the account balance, whichever is less. The rest is paid now.
- $50 non-refundable enrollment fee per term, due at sign-up.
- The deferred amount is drafted automatically on the scheduled installment date from a checking or savings account or a credit card, set up during enrollment.
- Eligible with at least $300 of deferrable balance left after financial aid is applied, and no payment restriction or other financial hold.
- Enroll in PantherPay through PAWS. Missing an installment removes you from the plan and can lead to withdrawal from Georgia State.
- Georgia State says there is no other installment option (state law and Board of Regents policy).

## Office of Financial Aid (demo)
- Email: finaid@gsu.example
- Verification documents are uploaded in the Financial Aid Portal under "Required Documents".

## Emergency grant (demo)
Up to $500 for students facing an unexpected expense (rent, medical, travel home, lost income). Apply through the Dean of Students online form; decisions within 2 business days. It does not need to be repaid.

## Financial aid appeal, special circumstances (demo)
If your family's income dropped or changed since the tax year on your FAFSA (job loss, medical costs, divorce, death in the family), ask the aid office for a Special Circumstances Review. The office can adjust your aid. Ask for an extension on verification documents at the same time if you need it.
